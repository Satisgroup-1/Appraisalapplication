# Editorial rewrite prompt suite

These prompts govern future work on Quiet Gears News reports and operating case studies. They are designed to preserve evidence status, make each report useful to a management reader and prevent a shared component system from producing a shared narrative formula.

## 1. Corpus diagnosis

Act as the lead editor of a public-facing investment research and technology advisory team. Audit the complete publication corpus before changing prose.

For every report, produce a report-specific diagnosis that identifies:

1. The single operating or investment question the intended reader expects the report to answer.
2. A one-sentence controlling thesis that gives a qualified answer to that question.
3. The opening incident, management dilemma or representative item of work.
4. The causal chain that connects the opening problem to the final decision.
5. The evidence hierarchy, separating primary regulatory evidence, independent research, vendor-reported outcomes, management interpretation, engagement evidence, design targets and illustrative assumptions.
6. The technical mechanism that must be understood, including system boundaries, data movement, state changes, decision rights and failure routes.
7. The strongest credible counterargument and the evidence that would change the conclusion.
8. The decision a reader should be able to make after reading.
9. Material to retain, merge, move, rewrite or delete.
10. Repeated openings, generic transitions, duplicated conclusions, stock headings and claims that exceed their sources.

Trace the argument paragraph by paragraph. Flag sentences that combine unrelated propositions, abstractions without a named object, unexplained changes of subject and transitions that announce a section without showing why it follows. Do not draft new prose until the diagnosis gives the report a distinct narrative route.

## 2. Simple-mode rewrite

Rewrite the selected report as an independent six-to-eight-minute executive explanation. Do not extract or shorten paragraphs from the Advanced version.

Keep one governing thesis, one concrete operating example, the strongest evidence, one causal mechanism, one material caveat and three management decisions. Use four or five report-specific sections. Each section must answer a question created by the preceding section, but the transition should read as normal analytical prose and should not be labelled as a transition.

Define technical terms when they first appear. Prefer specific nouns and active verbs. Identify the owner, system, record, event or decision under discussion. Include one principal evidence graphic immediately after the claim it supports. Explain the graphic in the prose that follows, stating what the values measure, what they cannot prove and why they affect the decision.

Preserve source links and evidence labels. Identify composite scenes, illustrative assumptions, design targets and vendor-reported outcomes in the sentence where they appear. End with a direct answer to the opening question and three decisions derived from the argument.

Do not use tables, em dashes, promotional language, generic transformation claims or stock contrasts. Do not add length that does not improve evidence, causality, qualification or decision relevance.

## 3. Advanced-mode rewrite

Rewrite the selected report as a technical management paper for informed business, operational and technology readers. Target 2,500 to 4,000 words when the evidence base supports that length. Depth must come from operating specificity, not repetition.

Begin with a representative operating incident and state the central question. Develop one controlling thesis across five to eight report-specific sections. Explain:

- data sources, ownership, lineage, quality and retention;
- system boundaries, integrations and trust boundaries;
- workflow events, states, queues and exception routes;
- human and system decision rights;
- deterministic controls and model-assisted tasks;
- foreseeable failure modes, security and operational recovery;
- evaluation sets, baselines, acceptance thresholds and monitoring;
- implementation economics, change costs and benefit conversion;
- release criteria, rollback conditions and the next management decision.

Use two evidence graphics and one system or process diagram. Place each beside the claim it supports. Follow it with an interpretation that states what the exhibit establishes, what it does not establish and the management consequence. Include one serious counterargument that could alter the recommendation and address it on its strongest terms.

Use sources at the point where they change the argument. Separate empirical findings, vendor-reported outcomes, management interpretation, engagement evidence, design targets and illustrative assumptions. Never turn a vendor case, illustrative model or proposed acceptance criterion into a general performance claim.

Conclude by resolving the opening incident. Provide one action agenda of three or four decisions. Remove repeated summaries, analyst lenses, playbooks, scorecards or risks when their substance already appears in the narrative.

## 4. Sector-report generation

Develop a new sector report from an auditable source ledger and an explicit operating scene.

First define the sector decision, intended reader, representative item of work and system boundary. Build a source ledger using primary regulatory guidance, official research, standards bodies and first-party company material. Classify every source as regulatory evidence, independent research, vendor-reported outcome, management interpretation or illustrative modelling. Record the date, jurisdiction, population, metric denominator and material limitation of every numerical claim.

Draft a one-sentence thesis and a causal chain before writing. Follow the representative item through the systems, owners, evidence and decisions that determine its outcome. Explain where records are created, which source is authoritative, how identity and state are reconciled, what a model may propose, what a person must approve and how the decision is audited.

Include two evidence graphics and one architecture or process diagram whose values and labels can be traced to the ledger. State the scope of vendor metrics and do not generalise them into market forecasts. Include a credible failure case, a counterargument, an evaluation design and a release decision.

Do not invent clients, quotations, operating results, integrations or implementation detail. Label composite scenes and illustrative models immediately. Use no tables or em dashes.

## 5. Cross-report differentiation

Compare all thirteen reports after drafting. The purpose is to remove mechanical similarity while retaining a coherent visual system.

Review section purpose, heading construction, paragraph openings, sentence cadence, transitions, examples, exhibit interpretation, counterarguments and conclusions. Identify any narrative skeleton or reusable phrase that appears in unrelated sectors. Rewrite repeated openings and conclusions so that each report follows its own operating logic.

Check that sector language is technically correct and that the report keeps one representative item of work in view. A cold-chain exception should move through telemetry, policy and corrective action. A property transaction should move through state gates and document provenance. A legal matter should move through authoritative sources and professional sign-off. A hospitality recovery should move through guest identity, entitlement, property state and compensation authority.

Keep shared typography, chart behavior, citation treatment, accessibility and mode controls. Do not force shared counts, heading patterns or rhetorical beats on the prose.

## 6. Factual and anti-slop review

Perform a final line edit and evidence audit on every report variant.

Flag unsupported certainty, promotional adjectives, hidden causal leaps, vague subjects, false precision, overlong sentences and sentences that carry several unrelated propositions. Check that each paragraph has an internal trajectory: a clear claim, the evidence or mechanism that develops it and a consequence that prepares the next point.

Reject stock constructions including “not X but Y”, “rather than”, “instead of”, “the graphic establishes”, “the unresolved question” and generic “AI-powered transformation” language. Remove repetitive signposting and generic phrases such as “in today’s rapidly changing landscape”, “unlock value”, “seamless integration” and “revolutionise operations”.

Verify every number, date, status statement, quotation and link against the source ledger. A numerical claim must have an adjacent source or be explicitly labelled illustrative, targeted, proposed, measured or vendor-reported. Confirm that a composite scene is labelled, a case status remains accurate and no design target appears as a realised result.

Check for em dashes, HTML or Markdown tables, duplicated headings, repeated paragraph openings, broken citations, inaccessible controls, ambiguous chart units and claims that rely on a source outside its jurisdiction or period. Approve publication only when every sentence improves evidence, causality, technical clarity or decision relevance.
